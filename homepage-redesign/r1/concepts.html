Phase 2: Three Directions

All three share the same foundation: the unified button system, AA color pairings (brand-tokens §6), Oswald/Helvetica, one H1, and a role-based type scale. Sponsor a Child is the one filled primary action in every hero. The concepts differ in what organizes the page: A is organized around time (a relationship over years), B around a tool (finding a child to sponsor), and C around people (which kind of partner you are). Each one also proposes a different menu structure, so you can choose the menu separately from the layout.

A. The Journey

Thesis: Sponsorship isn't a transaction. It's the first step in a relationship that can take you to meet your sponsored child, and back home as an ambassador.

  • Leads with: Love (relationship), then Joy (visiting, sharing)
  • Designed for: first-time and younger supporters who arrive from social media or a friend on a phone, and need to feel what they're joining before they act
  • Drives: Sponsor a Child. Then, in order, the next steps of the journey: trip interest, getting involved, becoming an ambassador and sharing, and partnering at scale
  • Page structure: a portrait hero with a single ask → a five-step journey rail (Sponsor, Visit, Serve, Share, Partner) that becomes a sticky progress indicator as you scroll → one chapter per step, each with its own real content (trips, Walk 4 Water, ambassadors, Lee Family Foundation) → pillars → social proof → footer
  • Menu (IA option 1, familiar): Sponsor · Programs · Trips · Give · About. A mega menu with a photo card in each panel
  • Solves audit problems: #2 (a single primary action, with everything else sequenced as "next"), #5 (short mobile hero, sticky sponsor bar, horizontal chapters with a peek), and the journey gap
  • HIG / M3 principles: HIG Depth (a layered narrative, with the rail showing where you are) and Deference (full-bleed photos lead); M3 Expressive (display type for chapter numbers) and emphasized-decelerate motion on section entry (with a reduced-motion fallback)

B. Find Your Match

Thesis: Make sponsoring a child as clear and quick as the best apps people already use, and still personal.

  • Leads with: Dignity (every child is known by name and story), then Health
  • Designed for: ready-to-act visitors on a phone, including people coming back from a church talk, an event or a QR code, who want to sponsor now
  • Drives: Sponsor a Child, with the fewest steps. Secondary: this season's program highlight (Pathways to Purpose), events, and crisis relief
  • Page structure: a compact hero with a sponsorship finder in the first view (segmented filters: country and age; the child cards are placeholders) → "How sponsorship works" in 3 steps with the monthly [AMOUNT] → a highlights row (Pathways to Purpose, Walk 4 Water, Crisis Relief: Nepal) → pillar tiles as the impact dashboard → social proof → footer. On mobile there's a bottom action bar (the M3 compact pattern) and a mega menu as a full-height sheet
  • Menu (IA option 2, task-based): "I want to…": Sponsor a child · Give · Go on a trip · Get involved · Learn about H4KI
  • Solves audit problems: #2 (the conversion tool is the hero), #5 (mobile-first components: chips, cards, bottom bar), #1 (program colors as chips with black labels), #3 (menus written as tasks)
  • HIG / M3 principles: HIG Direct manipulation (filtering updates results in place) and Feedback (visible selected states, a live result count); M3 filter chips, segmented buttons, cards on a surface-container ladder, and a 48dp minimum target

C. Partners in Hope

Thesis: H4KI restores hope through four pillars, and there's a clear, dignified way for everyone to partner in it: individuals, churches and foundations or companies.

  • Leads with: all four pillars, built as a visible framework (Dignity, Health, Joy, Love), with programs mapped to each
  • Designed for: church leaders, family foundation and corporate giving officers, major gift prospects, and loyal supporters doing their homework, mostly on desktop
  • Drives: Sponsor a Child for individuals (still the primary filled CTA), plus Start a church partnership and Discuss a project partnership, with the Lee Family Foundation partnership as proof
  • Page structure: a typographic hero that names the promise, with a photo mosaic → a "How will you partner?" three-lane router (Individuals & families / Churches / Foundations & companies) → a pillars framework (each pillar → programs → a proof point) → a project partnership feature (Lee Preparatory Academy and Nyafumba Village) → trust (Charity Navigator, Mission Excellence, financials) → social proof → footer
  • Menu (IA option 3, audience-based): Our Work · Sponsor · Churches · Foundations & Companies · About. The mega menu shows program by pillar
  • Solves audit problems: #3 (the menu answers "is there a place for me?"), #4 (strict editorial type scale), #2 (clear lanes instead of 20 equal buttons), and it moves trust signals up from the footer
  • HIG / M3 principles: HIG Clarity and Consistency (a strong grid, uniform terms); M3 type scale used strictly by role, tonal surfaces (surface-container-low/high) instead of shadows for depth, and the Adaptive layout moving from 3 columns to 1
