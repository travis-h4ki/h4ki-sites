Recommendation

Build on A. The Journey. Put B's finder into its first chapter and use C's partner paths in the menu.

Why A: it's the only direction whose structure is the strategy you described: sponsor, visit, serve, share, partner. It keeps one filled "Sponsor a child" action in the hero, the header and a sticky mobile bar, then shows every other goal (trips, Walk 4 Water, ambassadors, foundation partnerships) as the next step rather than a competing button. That directly fixes the biggest audit problem. It works best on phones and for the younger supporters you want to reach, and it scored highest on primary CTA clarity.

What to borrow: B's filterable child finder is the strongest conversion mechanism of the three. It belongs in Chapter 01 as soon as a live child feed is confirmed (see Open items). C's audience lanes and "Churches" and "Foundations & companies" menu entries give church and major gift prospects a clear "place for me" that A's familiar menu doesn't. Add them as a Partner panel in A's mega menu.

Elementor fit: A and C can be built with native Elementor containers, the Menu widget's mega menu, sticky and anchor settings, and Loop Carousel. B's finder needs a data source (a sponsorship feed from the new CRM stack or a Givecloud embed), so it's the only piece that needs custom work.

A. The Journey

Strengths

  • Shows the whole supporter lifecycle, not just one gift
  • Strongest emotional tone; the portrait hero keeps the current photo DNA
  • Most mobile ready: full-bleed hero and a sticky sponsor bar

Trade-offs

  • Long page; each chapter needs fresh content (trips, events) kept up to date
  • Foundations only see their path in Chapter 05, far down the page
  • Journey claims (visiting your child) need program confirmation

B. Find Your Match

Strengths

  • Fewest steps to sponsor; the tool is the hero
  • Most app-like and mobile-native (chips, bottom bar)
  • Clear seasonal highlights slot (Pathways to Purpose, crisis)

Trade-offs

  • Needs live child data, and child photos on the homepage need a privacy and consent policy
  • Feels transactional if the stories behind the cards are thin
  • Weakest for churches and major gifts

C. Partners in Hope

Strengths

  • Best for church and foundation growth, with trust signals moved up the page
  • Pillars become a visible framework (pillar to programs to proof)
  • Easiest to build natively in Elementor

Trade-offs

  • Sponsorship shares attention with two other lanes
  • Editorial and desktop-leaning; less urgency on phones
  • Pillar to program mapping needs sign-off from the program team
