Homepage Audit: hope4kidsinternational.org (Sep 25 2026)

Method. Headless Chrome at 1440×900 and 390×844 (screenshots in screenshots/live/). Styles are computed values from the DOM, hover and focus states come from real pointer and keyboard events, and brand values come from the Elementor kit. Guidance is from the Apple HIG skill and the Material 3 skill (DesignReferences/apple-hig, material-design-3).
Scope. Both guideline sets are written for native apps. For a marketing website I've applied their platform-neutral principles (hierarchy, type scale, spacing rhythm, contrast, targets, motion, adaptive layout) and skipped app-only guidance (SwiftUI components, Liquid Glass materials, M3 dynamic color from a seed, which would override the brand palette).
Baseline. The primary goal is Sponsor a Child conversions, followed by program and event highlights. Secondary goals are major gifts, mission trips and crisis relief. The strategic arc is the relationship journey: sponsor, visit, engage, ambassador, share, and partner at scale.

Top 5 strengths

  1. A distinctive, ownable button language.
    The pill with an offset 1/2/2/1 black border reads as hand drawn and warm, and it's used on every CTA.
    M3 Shape: corner-full for primary actions. HIG Consistency: one recognizable control style people learn once.
  2. Photography that shows real faces, at human scale.
    The hero close-up and the classroom and water photos put children first and never feel staged. The speckle overlay adds texture without hiding the person.
    HIG Deference: let content set the mood, and keep chrome from competing with it.
  3. Primary action is always in the header.
    "Sponsor a Child" is the orange filled pill at top right on desktop and one of the two top buttons on mobile, so it's always one tap away.
    HIG Clarity, M3 emphasis hierarchy: one high-emphasis filled button for the key action.
  4. A strong, coherent type pairing.
    Condensed Oswald headlines with a neutral Helvetica body give a confident, editorial voice that feels youthful, and the pairing matches M3 Expressive's "brand display font + neutral sans" model.
    M3 Typography: expressive font for display and headline, neutral sans for body.
  5. The 4 Pillars and a sense of place are already in the content.
    The mission names Dignity, Health, Joy and Love. Trips, countries and programs are concrete, and the page shows a real partnership (Lee Family Foundation) and real events (Walk 4 Water).
    HIG Metaphors and Clarity: concrete, familiar objects make the offer understandable.

Top 5 problems

  1. Color contrast fails WCAG AA on core navigation.
    Program pills put colored text on white: Medical and Trips #92CAB4 1.8:1, Education #FFA225 2.0:1, Water #2196F3 3.1:1, Sponsorship #F24444 3.7:1. "Learn More" and "All Current Events" are orange on white (2.0:1). The crisis hover is white on red (3.7:1). The status of each program is signaled by color alone.
    HIG Accessibility → Contrast (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large and UI) and "Differentiate Without Color." M3 Color → always pair a container with its on-* role.
    Fix: program colors become fills and dots with black text (5.7 to 11.3:1); pair color with a label or icon.
  2. The primary goal isn't the primary action above the fold.
    The hero currently sells a single campaign. The mobile fold shows "Say Yes Today" (campaign), and Sponsor a Child only appears as a small outline button in the header. Below the fold there are more than 20 buttons (including 12 carousel "View Event" pills), most with the same visual weight, so sponsorship competes with events, trips, water and donate.
    HIG Clarity (one clear focal point per screen). M3 emphasis ladder: filled → tonal → outlined → text, with only one filled action per view.
    Fix: hero states the lasting offer (sponsor a child); campaigns go into a flexible "highlight" slot; one filled button per section.
  3. Navigation follows the org chart, not the supporter.
    About, Programs, Countries, Give and Get Involved hold about 35 links. The same destination lives in two places (Sponsor a Child under Give plus a header button). There's no path for churches or foundations and corporations, even though those are strategic growth goals. Desktop and mobile menus have different items and order, and the desktop Give menu has a typo ("Cicle").
    HIG Consistency (uniform terminology and structure across sizes). M3 Adaptive: the same information architecture, a different container per window size. HIG Patterns: organize by the task people came to do.
    Fix: a mega menu organized by intent (Sponsor, Give, Go, Partner, About) with the same tree on mobile.
  4. Type scale and heading structure are inconsistent.
    The page has two H1s ("A Legacy of Hope…" and "UPCOMING TRIPS") and an H2 that's only a 24px subtitle ("and would love for you…"). H2 sizes are 74, 44 and 24px. H3s (country names) are 44px, the same as H2s. The "Safe Water Saves Lives" H3 is 74px, bigger than its parent H2. Body text has 1px letter spacing and is centered in long blocks.
    M3 Typography: 15 roles, used by role, not by px. HIG Typography: semantic text styles, hierarchy through size and weight. WCAG 1.3.1 (info and relationships).
    Fix: a single role-based scale (display, headline, title, body, label) with one H1 and ordered headings.
  5. Mobile is a shrunk desktop, not a mobile-first flow.
    On 390px the hero takes about 1.5 screens before any sponsorship content. The program pills row is hidden on mobile. Four event carousels stack with dots as the only navigation. No sponsorship action follows the user down the page, and the floating accessibility button covers the hero CTA. Impact counters sit in three 100% wide cards that say "1 / 0 mil / 1" until they animate. Meanwhile, the mobile audience and a younger supporter base are both strategic goals.
    M3 Adaptive (compact window: bottom-anchored primary actions, content before chrome). HIG Layout (safe areas, controls clear of overlays), HIG Feedback (no state that looks broken before animation).
    Fix: a short hero, a sticky bottom "Sponsor a child" bar on mobile, horizontal scroll with a peek instead of dots, counters that render real values without JavaScript.

Other findings (for the mockups and the live site)

  • Motion: every button uses scale(1.1) over 500ms. That's slow, and it overlaps neighbors (see brand-tokens §6a). There's no prefers-reduced-motion handling. Elementor entrance animations hide content until scripts run.
  • Stale content: the Mexico trip (Jan 14 to 16, 2026) is past. The Founder's Day hero and its "Over fifty years ago" copy are still live.
  • Voice: "suffering from extreme poverty" (mission block) should become "children living in poverty." "Safe Water Saves Lives" uses "saves" as an org claim; the voice guide recommends "restore" or "transform." "Lives Saved" as a counter has the same issue.
  • Trust signals (Charity Navigator four-star badge, Mission Excellence associate member) are buried in the footer; they deserve a place near the major-gift and partner asks.
  • Journey gaps: nothing connects sponsorship to trips ("visit your sponsored child"), to becoming an ambassador, or to sharing. The relationship journey from the strategy doesn't show up on the page.
