Hope 4 Kids International · Homepage redesign, round 2

Three homepage directions shaped by the board

Prepared September 30, 2026, updated October 1 with Travis's answers, for the Development and Marketing team

These mockups respond to the direction in the May 2026 H4KI Board Packet, the Q3 2026 Board Update and the Hope 4 Kids Ministry Update (Sept 2026). The board is focused on long-term sustainability through partnerships, especially child sponsorship and operating support for the Eggum Family Medical Center; on reaching the next generation of supporters in the US with cause-first messaging; on turning first gifts into ongoing relationships; and on growing through companies, foundations, universities, churches, Ambassadors and Walk 4 Water. Every mockup leads with the Pathways to Purpose campaign in a seasonal slot, adds a general Donate button, keeps the program pills, events and trips, and replaces the menus with a mega menu. Each one makes a different bet about who the homepage should serve first.

Recommendation

Build on Mockup A, Pocket Stories (36 of 40). It is the only direction that keeps the approved $93 ask within thumb reach on every phone size, with price cards right after the stories and a sticky Sponsor / Donate bar, and it hits the board's Next Generation strategy and growth channels hardest. That matches what you asked for on Sep 25 (mobile first, younger supporters, Sponsor a Child as the main goal) and today's call to lead with Pathways to Purpose in a seasonal slot. Its intent-based mega menu (Sponsor, Give, Go, Partner, About) is also the clearest answer to the directors' request for a full nav overhaul.

Mockup C (35) is the closest alternative: the best supporter-journey idea, now fully sourced, and the lightest content load, but its five equal doors split attention from the campaign ask and it has no story or share moment for Next Gen visitors. Mockup B (33) is the most familiar and the easiest native build, but without the 2026 list it is a conventional, long page with no share or Ambassador path.

For the mid November deadline, launch the A base with the menu, hero template, program pills, price cards, stories with real photos, the people tiles, trip and walk lists, B's who-we-are facts, C's journey section and the partners band. Add the partner wall once logos are approved, and video stories for the January "This is your year to change a life" campaign.

Hybrid option

  • A: hero, story rail, price cards, sticky mobile bar and intent-based mega menu (the base)
  • B: "Who we are, in 20 seconds" with Since 1973, 8 countries and 1,397 wells, right after the program pills, for first-time visitors and search
  • C: "After you say yes" journey, now sourced from the live sponsorship pages, so it can ship at launch
  • C: tiered partner wall, once at least 3 partners approve their logos

Trade-offs

A. Pocket Stories

Gains

  • Fastest path from a phone to a gift: price cards and a sticky Sponsor / Give bar
  • Built for sharing: every story card has a Share button, which seeds Ambassador missions
  • Strongest fit with the Next Gen strategy and the 25 to 35 audience

Gives up

  • Less depth for foundations, companies and long-time partners doing homework
  • Desktop feels lighter than the other two

Risks

  • Only works if someone feeds it fresh short video and photos every month
  • Story cards with placeholder photos look empty until real assets land

Content and assets needed

  • 6 to 8 story assets (photo or 15 to 30 second captioned video) per season; the Dropbox Videos folder already has many
  • Brian photo files from the Sep 23 email
  • Grow the TikTok account (@hope4kidsint) if it becomes a main channel
  • Partner and university logos when ready

B. Open Book

Gains

  • Answers who H4KI is, what it does and where in the first scroll
  • Consistent public facts (Since 1973, 8 countries, 1,397 wells) help search and AI answers
  • Most familiar to today's visitors: it keeps the current hero card style

Gives up

  • Longest page, especially on mobile, before the second ask
  • Less emotional pull for a first-time Next Gen visitor

Risks

  • Without the 2026 list it has little that is new for returning visitors
  • Two Water 4 Kids lines still need the Director's sign off

Content and assets needed

  • Brian photo files
  • One vocational graduate story from the BVSC testimony videos
  • What $10,500 covers, from the Water 4 Kids Director
  • Partner logos when ready

C. Many Paths

Gains

  • Serves every audience with a clear door and one next step each
  • Makes the supporter journey visible, the board's growth engine
  • The path idea outlives any single campaign

Gives up

  • The hero ask competes with five doors right below it
  • Fewer stories and less proof than A or B

Risks

  • Journey copy has to stay in step with what the Sponsorship team actually delivers
  • A single "I want to..." menu is less conventional and needs a quick usability check

Content and assets needed

  • Brian photo files
  • Partner logos and permission by tier
  • A one-line description of the Where Most Needed fund
