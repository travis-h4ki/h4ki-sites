About this report

How to read this report

  • Every figure names its report in the sources line at the foot of each spread.
  • Pending marks a figure that is still being confirmed, such as a count that waits on the database move.
  • Interim, unaudited marks finance figures from internal statements through April; final 2026 statements will replace them.
  • Children are never named, and no caption pairs a child's face with a school name and a health condition.

Message from Leadership

Two messages from leadership

From the Executive Director

New ground, and deep roots

This year I began serving as Executive Director, and I do not take the responsibility lightly. God has led Hope 4 Kids International faithfully since 1973, and I am grateful to steward that mission with the 20 years of experience He used to prepare me. Thank you for the support, wisdom and encouragement you have given me through this leadership transition.

Our focus this season has been partnerships and growth that lasts, especially in Child Sponsorship and the annual operating funds of the Eggum Family Medical Center. In April the center opened to the community with a two day medical camp that served about 350 patients. It holds its official license to operate, runs an electronic medical record system, and has an operating model that includes a small part of each Uganda sponsorship.

There is much to celebrate. August was the highest giving month in our history, with $976K received, including our largest single day of giving ever, more than $536,000. Our first Founder's Day raised about $66,000 for the medical center. We are in the final stage of our move to a new supporter database, and Apple Pay is now a way to give.

There were hard moments too. We had to cancel our summer trips to Uganda, and our hearts have been heavy for several ministry partners around the world. Our partnerships are about so much more than buildings. They are relationships built over many years, and we keep our friends, their families and the communities they serve close in prayer.

Uganda is now Ebola free, and we are grateful to be moving forward with our November trip. Our team is eager to return to our Uganda staff, sponsored children, schools and projects. Across H4KI we are strengthening programs and relationships so we can serve more children and communities well in the years ahead.

Thank you for all you do for the children and families we serve.

Love well,Angie SimonExecutive Director

2026 Milestones

How the year unfolded

Ten moments in order, then the milestones the reports do not date. Months for the trips come from the trip photos where the reports give none.

  1. Jan

    The year's first team, in Mexico

    A January team served alongside our partners in Mexico, the first of the year's trips. Planning for a full summer of travel was already underway.

  2. Mar

    World Water Day, met and matched

    $109,563 funded 10 wells for villages we serve. In Nepal the second phase of a partner primary school was dedicated, and 150 water filtration systems arrived with the March team.

  3. Apr

    The medical center opens

    The Eggum Family Medical Center opened to the community with a two day camp for about 350 patients on April 10 and 11; it holds its official license to operate. A Sesera outreach with the April team served about 175 more. Supporters funded phase 3 of the Nepal school.

  4. May

    A first board meeting, two teams, hundreds of walkers

    The Executive Director's first board meeting in the role. Teams served in Guatemala and Romania. By the end of May, 753 people had walked in 6 Walk 4 Water events.

  5. Jun

    A hard decision

    We and our partners postponed the June Uganda trip. Summer trips to Romania, Guatemala and Mexico were set to go ahead.

  6. Jul

    Uganda canceled, Mexico served

    The July Uganda trip was canceled because of Ebola and insurance restrictions. A team led Vacation Bible School in Mexico, and the Brandom class of 87 was set to graduate.

  7. Aug

    The highest giving month in our history

    $976K given, including more than $536,000 in a single day. The first Founder's Day, planned for August 22 in the May packet, raised about $66,000 for the medical center. A team served in Namibia.

  8. Sep

    Cambodia and the Philippines

    Our first team to Cambodia was set to travel with the Philippines team, a combined trip meant to strengthen relationships and explore how we can keep growing our impact in that part of the world.

  9. Nov

    Back to Uganda

    Our third quarter update reports that Uganda has been declared Ebola free and the November trip is set to depart. The Executive Director's September update adds that the team is eager to spend time with our Uganda staff, sponsored children, schools and projects.

  10. Dec

    Year end board meeting

    December 4, 2026. Final 2026 figures will replace the interim numbers in this report.

Mission Teams

Serving Together

Amy BistaVP of Child Development and International Teams

By May 28, the year counted 49 team members so far and 4 completed trips. The summer brought full teams and two hard decisions about Uganda.

49team members so far in 2026, as of May 28

208team members in 2025 on 13 trips
49%of 2025 team members were traveling with us for the first time

Demand keeps growing

The May Guatemala and July Uganda trips filled quickly, and for the first time we set up a waitlist. A new Teams administrator joined us in January. Our summer began with trips to Romania and Guatemala that went well, and we hosted our first trip with a church youth group. In July a team led Vacation Bible School in Mexico, and in August a team served in Namibia.

Not everything went to plan

We and our partners made the joint decision to postpone the June Uganda trip after prayerful consideration. We have decades of experience navigating complex health and security situations in the field, and we have operated trips in Uganda during previous Ebola outbreaks. This situation required a different response. The July Uganda trip was later canceled because of Ebola and insurance restrictions. Our third quarter update reports that Uganda has been declared Ebola free, and the November trip is set to depart.

College partners

At a partner college, two pharmacy students on our Guatemala trip are developing a research project connected to their experience. We remain committed to our school partners for the long term, and we look forward to expanding partnerships with schools and youth groups.

“This decision was not made quickly or lightly”Teams department, H4KI board packet, May 2026

  1. Planning for 2027 is already underway, with strong interest in July Uganda, a university student medical trip to Uganda and an April Dress a Girl trip.

New places, new partners

Both of our new local partners in Cambodia are adding child sponsorship. In Uganda we partnered with a partner organization to provide trauma and abuse training to most of our staff who work directly with children, using an Africa specific curriculum. On our trips we will challenge team members to take an envelope with three children who need sponsors, with a goal of 100 children a year.

Checkups and urgent needs

Our Health Services team completed preventative checkups for every student at one of our primary schools, and we are following up on the needs they found. In June and July a dedicated campaign raised $6,301 to cover all 17 children.

The Higher Education campaign has raised $28,902 so far, covering more than the first year of university for five students, and our Sponsorship Manager in Uganda obtained a discount for some of our university students. The team also spent many hours training and planning for the database move, and is improving processes in Uganda so parents are better informed and students report test results quickly.

Staffing and systems

We hired an additional lab technician and a full time radiographer to lead X ray and ultrasound services, and we are recruiting a part time medical officer and clinical officer. With the Projects team we installed fans throughout the center, extended the laboratory counters, built a storage shed, completed pavers to the restrooms and put up a road sign for community visibility. We purchased an immunoassay analyzer to run more tests in house, a water bath for blood bank requirements and a phototherapy bed for newborn referrals. We installed surge protection and battery backups, and we launched an electronic medical record system in place of paper records.

Opening and outreach

We opened to the community on April 10 and 11, and the center holds its official license to operate; the next spread tells the story of that weekend. We completed health screenings for all the children at one of our primary schools and held a medical outreach in Sesera with the April team, serving about 175 patients. Transformational Story

The doors opened on a Friday

About350patients in two days, April 10 and 11, 2026
“Our new Eggum Family Medical Center is open and operational!”Executive Director, report to the board, May 2026

In December 2025 the Eggum Family Medical Center on the Uganda campus was fully staffed and its X ray and ultrasound equipment procured. The December minutes recorded four 2026 goals for it: finish licensing and registering the center and its equipment, complete baseline health exams for all sponsored children, open to the public, and explore partnerships.

On Friday, April 10, and Saturday, April 11, the center opened to the community with a two day medical camp. About 350 patients came through the doors in those two days. The center also holds its official license to operate. The same month the team held an outreach in Sesera with the April trip team and served about 175 more.

Opening the doors was one task. Keeping them open is a different one. The team launched an electronic medical record system to replace paper records, so that patient history, inventory and finances are tracked and no child's follow up is missed. It hired an additional lab technician and a full time radiographer, and with the Projects team fitted the building out for daily use. The Health Services spread lists the equipment.

The money to keep the doors open is being built the same way, one source at a time. Hope 4 Kids International adjusted its program allocations so a small part of each Uganda sponsorship supports checkups, which resulted in about $40,000 a year for operations. The first Founder's Day raised about $66,000 for the center. A hospital donated supplies, and by May a campaign for operating needs had raised its first gifts. In its first weeks the center generated about UGX 4,000,000 (about $1,150).

The center is also becoming part of its neighborhood. A borehole now serves the villages around the campus, with an invitation to local leaders to send sick community members to the center. A secondary school a few miles away received a large scale water project, and the team is building that relationship so its students come for care. Radio health talks carry the center's name across eastern Uganda, and partnerships with hospitals and clinics in the area are taking shape.

Next come a weekly health education radio program, registration as an official immunization site, health screenings for sponsored children at other schools, and more staff and specialty services. The doors opened on a Friday. The work of keeping them open has only begun.

Wells

By May 19 we had drilled 44 wells for the year, ahead of the 31 drilled by the same point in 2025. For World Water Day our goal was 5 wells at $52,500; a generous match covered 5 more, and the campaign raised $109,563, funding 10 wells for villages we serve.

Uganda

A large scale water project is underway at a large girls' secondary school in eastern Uganda. We supported the villages around the campus with a borehole, and we assessed a school in Masindi serving children with disabilities as a future project site. New funding is supporting deep water wells in Uganda. On the campus itself, work continues on wastewater infrastructure.

Beyond Uganda

We completed our 15th well in Cambodia since the work there began, with a local partner. In Nepal we brought 150 water filtration systems on the March trip as we start our next large scale water project there. In the Philippines the water program is relaunching: a deep well site has been identified and fundraising is underway. For Guatemala we planned 75 filtration systems for the 2026 trip.

Walk 4 Water

From January through May, 6 events drew 753 participants, raised $115,700 gross and funded 11 wells. One community walk brought in 3 wells, and another community walk raised funds for five more (date not reported). We convened walk leaders to share best practices and are redesigning the Event Guide to lower the barriers for first time hosts.

An honest look

Through May 19 our finances figure was $467,803, compared with $518,909 for the same window in 2025. Our May board page does not explain the difference, and we are confirming what the figure measures. In the interim, unaudited books, Well Projects contributions were $425,107 against a $433,333 budget for January to April.

    What happened

    We and our partners postponed the June Uganda trip, and the July trip was later canceled because of Ebola and insurance restrictions. Summer trips to Romania, Guatemala and Mexico went ahead.

    The third quarter update reports Uganda Ebola free; the November team is preparing to travel.

    By May 19, 44 wells had been drilled, ahead of 31 by the same point in 2025. Over the same window the Water 4 Kids International finances figure was $467,803, compared with $518,909 in 2025.

    The board page gives no reason for the difference. W4KI is confirming what the figure measures.

    What happened

    Funding higher education is an ongoing challenge, as the Child Sponsorship team reported in May.
